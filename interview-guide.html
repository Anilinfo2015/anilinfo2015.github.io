HLD and LLD interview questions

Every worked answer uses the same delivery sequence: requirements → core entities → API or system interface → high-level design → deep dives. A separate data-flow sketch is optional for processing pipelines. HLD applies the sequence to services and storage; LLD applies it to objects, methods, and state ownership.

One framework, two levels of design

StageSystem design (HLD)Low-level design (LLD)
1. RequirementsUser actions, latency, scale, durability, consistency, and scopeOperations, business rules, execution model, complexity, and scope
2. Core entitiesRecords, identities, and relationshipsObjects, responsibilities, state ownership, and invariants
3. API or system interfaceEndpoints, events, or job contractsPublic methods, results, errors, and retry semantics
Data flow (optional)Logical input-to-output pipeline before choosing componentsTransformations in a multi-stage component, only when useful
4. High-level designWorking baseline with end-to-end service and storage flowsWorking core logic and collaboration between objects
5. Deep divesBottlenecks, decision-driven estimates, scaling, recovery, and requirement checksTrade-offs, extensions, concurrency, failure handling, and concrete tests

Scope belongs inside requirements, not in a separate delivery stage. Estimates support a decision rather than delay the baseline. Choose deep dives from the requirements; there is no required number of them. Preserve correctness in the baseline before refining performance.

Coverage review

The starting library had five HLD case-study subjects spread across 41 files, plus 19 LLD worked articles and 11 LLD technique guides. HLD lacked short, comparable answers for messaging, feeds, storage, payments, booking, search, and background processing. LLD covered many familiar questions but mixed interview dialogue, implementations, and production discussion; library lending, rentals, inventory, file systems, scheduling, and order matching were missing.

This catalog brings those gaps into one structure: 30 HLD articles and 26 LLD articles. The longer HLD series expand individual stages, and the LLD technique guides coach delivery of the same sequence. Their existing detail remains available without imposing a different template on the worked answers.

These are common practice question families, not a frequency ranking or a claim to cover every employer’s question bank. Product names describe familiar prompts, not those companies’ actual implementations. Workload and latency figures are explicit interview assumptions.

Longer studies and interview technique

The short HLD answers supplement the existing series on URL shortening, distributed caching, web crawling, YouTube, tag management, and a distributed message queue. Chapter navigation identifies the delivery stage and links to a separate scoped walkthrough; the longer studies can make different workload assumptions.

The LLD interview playbook explains how to deliver the five stages. Its focused guides cover requirements, modeling, interfaces, working flows, and deep dives rather than introduce an alternative article structure.

Reference framework

Both templates use Hello Interview’s system-design delivery framework as their structural reference. The LLD mapping is this repository’s adaptation; the explanations and scoped solutions are original. No public source establishes a complete list of frequently asked questions.
