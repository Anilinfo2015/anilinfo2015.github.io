HLD interview article template

Start with a design that handles the requested features. Add scale and failure handling only after its limits are clear. Each worked article follows the sections below.

1. Requirements

  • Functional: three or four concrete user actions.
  • Non-functional: numbered targets for latency, scale, consistency, durability, or availability. Treat numbers as interview assumptions, not measured product statistics.

2. Out of scope

Name the features excluded from this design. Authentication and resource authorization still apply to every API; an identity platform need not be designed here.

3. Core entities

List the records, their keys, and the relationships needed by the main flows. Include the invariant that must not break.

4. API

Show operations, important inputs, responses, and relevant errors. Specify pagination, ownership checks, and retry behavior where they affect correctness.

5. Basic design

Use a small Mermaid diagram and a numbered read/write flow. This baseline must cover every functional requirement; it does not yet need to meet the scale targets.

6. Trade-offs and bottlenecks

Explain what the baseline makes easy and where it fails. Compare a concrete alternative, rather than listing technologies.

7. Scale estimate

State assumptions, units, arithmetic, and the decision affected by the result. Separate average from peak load and raw data from indexes and replicas. Do not calculate numbers that change nothing.

8. Deep dive 1

Address the first group of non-functional requirements: mechanism, partition key or contention boundary, failure behavior, and cost.

9. Deep dive 2

Address the remaining non-functional requirements. Explain retries, recovery, and the consistency guarantee instead of claiming “exactly once” without a boundary.

10. Check the design

Map every numbered non-functional requirement to one of the two deep dives. Include a failure scenario and an observable result. A target is a goal to test, not a guarantee created by drawing a cache.

Sources and review

The section order draws on Hello Interview’s delivery framework: requirements, core entities, API, a working high-level design, then deep dives. These are original worked designs, not copies of its solutions. The framework was checked through indexed search results on September 23, 2026; direct page access was unavailable.
